grip Software Paper Citation Verification

Audit date: 2026-09-02. Every citation used in grip-software-paper.Rmd has a matching BibTeX record and a primary or official source that supports the cited manuscript claim. Numerical benchmark results are generated by the manuscript or loaded from package artifacts and are not attributed to the literature sources.

The conditional weighted-core bound in Computational boundaries is an analysis in Supplement S1, not a theorem attributed to the original GRIP papers. Sections S1.3--S1.6 provide the ball-overlap identity, cache accounting, explicit insertion coverage assumption, telescoping insertion bound, compressed-path counterexample, explicit expected-draw formula, and conditional expected-time and memory estimates. Bounded degree, bounded overlap, coarse-vertex coverage, bounded adjacent level-size ratios, logarithmic depth, and fixed tuning parameters are required for the stated time bound; geometric contraction is additionally required for linear storage. This claim mapping and the analyzed source files were checked on 2026-09-02. The supplement's independent finite mathematical checks pass; they are not package timing benchmarks or a proof of an asymptotic bound. No new scalability experiment is reported. The supplement has its own citation evidence.

gajer2002verified Introduction, GRIP implementation and contribution paragraphs; computational boundaries The original GRIP system uses recursive coarsening, intelligent placement, and computationally localized force-directed refinement, with KK-style forces at intermediate filtration levels and Fruchterman--Reingold refinement at the final level. Its force calculations use a fixed unit edge length. For bounded-degree graphs, the paper expresses standard MIS-filtration time through the sizes of truncated BFS trees and states that no subquadratic construction bound was known. Gajer and Kobourov. Section 3, pp. 207--208, gives the bounded-degree filtration-time expression in terms of partial BFS trees, reports near-linear empirical time and a contribution below 3% in the experiments, gives n + log delta(G) entries for the stored filtration, and distinguishes this evidence from a proved subquadratic bound. Section 4, p. 211, defines the localized KK and FR forces and states that edgeLength is the unit edge length. gajer2004verified Introduction, GRIP implementation paragraph; core layout API, higher-dimensional motivation; computational boundaries The GRIP method is hierarchical, uses a maximal-independent-set filtration, supports embeddings in arbitrary Euclidean dimension, and projects higher-dimensional embeddings to 2D or 3D. The original study reports smoother, more symmetric projected drawings; its Möbius-strip examples compare direct 3D layouts with 4D layouts projected to 3D. For bounded-degree graphs, after filtration construction, its time complexity is Theta(n k^2) and its space complexity is Theta(n k), with k = log delta(G) for an MIS filtration and k = log n for a graph-centers filtration. Gajer, Goodrich, and Kobourov. A multi-dimensional approach to force-directed layouts of large graphs. Computational Geometry 29(1), 3-18, 2004. DOI 10.1016/j.comgeo.2004.03.014. Coauthor-hosted full article PDF; publisher DOI Section 3.1 (pp. 7--8) defines the filtration and its coarse-to-fine placement/refinement phases; Section 3.2 (pp. 9--10) defines the maximal-independent-set filtration. Section 3.6 (pp. 13--14) gives the arbitrary-dimensional projection procedure. Figures 5--6 (p. 14) compare Möbius strips on 150, 300, and 1500 vertices, drawn directly in 3D or drawn in 4D and projected to 3D; the captions distinguish rough and smooth twists. The abstract reports improved smoothness and symmetry. This supports a historical illustrative observation, not a guarantee of improved distance fidelity or a claim that the current R implementation has reproduced the experiment. The Main Theorem in Section 3.7 (p. 15) states the exact conditional time and space bounds and defines k for both filtration families; the conclusion explicitly identifies MIS-filtration construction complexity as excluded and unresolved. Verified against the complete Computational Geometry article, using the coauthor-hosted published PDF; Section 3.6 and Figures 5--6 were read and the figure page visually inspected on 2026-09-02.
