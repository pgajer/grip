# GKK LGKK Paper Outline

Date: 2026-04-03

## Working Title

Primary option:

`Geodesic Kamada-Kawai for Weighted Graph Drawing: Full Path-Based Stress, a Landmark-Sparse Approximation, and Multiscale Integration`

Shorter option:

`Geodesic Kamada-Kawai for Weighted Graph Drawing`

## One-Sentence Thesis

Classical KK matches graph distances to straight-line chords; this paper replaces the chord term by embedded path length, introduces a landmark-sparse approximation, and shows how the same geodesic objective can be used inside a MISF-based multiscale pipeline.

## Scope

This paper should do three things well:

1. Define full `GKK` as the clean path-based analogue of classical `KK`.
2. Define `LGKK` as the practical sparse approximation to `GKK`.
3. Show how `GKK/LGKK` ideas connect to multiscale `MISF` filtration and weighted `GRIP` as the scaling story.

This paper should not try to be:

- the full package paper
- the full geodesic MDS paper
- a paper whose main claim is that weighted GRIP alone beats classical GRIP

## Main Claims

### Claim 1

For weighted geometric graphs, classical `KK` can mismatch graph-metric targets and the realized geometry because it only measures straight-line chord distance.

Status:

- Already in place conceptually in `dev/papers/gkk_lgkk_paper/notes/concepts/geodesic_kk_functional/`
- Needs a compact journal-style motivation example and figure

### Claim 2

Replacing the chord term by embedded path length gives a coherent all-pairs geodesic objective, `GKK`, with an analytically profiled scale parameter and a usable gradient-based optimizer.

Status:

- Already in place in `dev/papers/gkk_lgkk_paper/notes/concepts/geodesic_kk_algorithm_implementation_plan/`
- Already implemented in `R/grip_quality.R`
- Needs manuscript-ready derivation, proposition formatting, and complexity presentation

### Claim 3

`LGKK` captures most of the practical benefit of `GKK` at much lower computational cost by restricting the pair set to local neighbors and a small set of landmark-linked pairs.

Status:

- Already in place in `dev/papers/gkk_lgkk_paper/notes/concepts/landmark_geodesic_kk_spec_2026-03-30/`
- Already implemented in `R/grip_quality.R`
- Already supported by the mesh benchmark report
- Needs a cleaner theoretical statement of what is being approximated

### Claim 4

The same geodesic correction can be used inside a `MISF`-based multiscale pipeline, linking `GKK/LGKK` to a weighted version of `GRIP` rather than presenting weighted GRIP as an unrelated standalone method.

Status:

- Already in place in the C++ multiscale implementation and weighted GRIP experiments
- Needs careful framing so it stays a scaling/application section, not a second competing paper inside this one

### 8. Experiments I: Weighted Mesh Benchmark

Purpose:

- present the strongest current evidence section first
- compare `KK`, `KK -> GKK`, and `KK -> LGKK`
- report quality-runtime tradeoffs in 2D and 3D

Already in place:

- benchmark script `tools/benchmarks/gkk_lgkk_paper/benchmark-kk-gkk-lgkk-mesh-suite.R`

- benchmark data under `output/benchmarks/kk-gkk-lgkk-mesh-suite-2026-03-31/`

Current strongest result:

- in 3D, mean full-GKK relative RMSE drops from about `0.0254` for `KK` to about `0.0067` for `KK -> GKK`, while `KK -> LGKK` reaches about `0.0069` at roughly one-seventh of the runtime of full `GKK`

The framing should be:

### 11. Discussion and Limitations

Purpose:

- say plainly what the paper does and does not establish

Key points to include:

- full `GKK` is expensive and best viewed as the reference objective
- `LGKK` captures most of the gain much more cheaply
- current evidence is strongest for weighted geometric graphs
- multiscale weighted `GRIP` is promising, but the paper's main thesis remains `GKK + LGKK`

### 12. Conclusion

Close with:

- path-based stress is a meaningful alternative to chord-based `KK`
- `LGKK` is a useful sparse approximation
- `MISF`-based multiscale integration gives the method a scalable home inside `GRIP`
